# (2) Design explicitly for your users and their needs

Engage meaningfully with diverse stakeholders throughout the process so that technology actually meets their needs and delivers real impact.&#x20;

* Talk to partners and users before design starts to understand what they actually need
* Base decisions on direct user insight and testing, not on internal assumptions about what you think users want
* Build collaborative design practices — workshops, feedback loops, co-design sessions — into every stage of the technology lifecycle, not just the beginning
* Actively seek out and include the voices most often left out, especially marginalised users and beneficiaries
* Revisit and re-test with users as the technology evolves, since needs and context can shift after launch



**Questions to ask yourself:**&#x20;

* Do you know who your users are and do you have access to them?&#x20;
* Where in your current project timeline do users first get consulted and is that early enough to actually shape the design?&#x20;
* Is there a group you've assumed you understand well enough to design for without asking them?
* Are co-design sessions and feedback loops built into your workflow by default, or do they happen only when someone remembers to schedule them?
