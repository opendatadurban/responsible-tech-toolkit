# (5) Employ strong security practices to safeguard user data

Surveillance is the ability of an entity to observe people's activities, communications, location, or behavior through the data trail they generate online, often continuously and without their express awareness about it. The concept of "Privacy by Design" is about preserving individual autonomy and agency in a world where institutions increasingly have the means to observe, predict, and influence behavior at scale. It is about helping users to maintain control over information about themselves. "Privacy by Design" also rejects the false choice between security and functionality. Thoughtful design makes it possible to achieve both at once where you can build tools that satisfy user needs and privacy requirements simultaneously, without trade-offs.&#x20;

Treat data protection as a design decision, not a compliance tickbox. Ensure that you think carefully through your user data pipeline from collection, use, storage and deletion.&#x20;

* Collect data ethically, and only if you have a genuine, defined use for it&#x20;
* Apply data minimisation as a protection measure. The safest data is the data you never collected in the first place
* Obtain informed consent, where users have a real, understandable choice over how their data is used. Respect user preferences. &#x20;
* Tell users clearly where their data comes from and how it's being used
* If you do share, anonymise or aggregate before analysis and sharing. Strip out or aggregate any details that could identify individual respondents.&#x20;
* Set clear data retention and termination policies, and weigh the value of keeping data (for provenance, record-keeping, accountability) against the risk of holding onto it too long
* Maintain secure, reliable systems, and revisit them over time where vulnerabilities and risks change as the technology and its context evolve<br>

**Questions to ask yourself:**&#x20;

* Are we actively using and analysing the data we collect to guide to decisions or are we collecting "just in case"?&#x20;
* How long are we keeping this data, and do we have an automated process to "forget" (delete) it once its purpose is served?
* Have we tested our data against "re-identification" (can it be linked back to a real person)?
