# (3) Design with the most marginalised in mind

Build technology where the people most likely to be excluded have real input into the design so that the result is inclusive and usable for everyone, regardless of ability, background, gender or social status.

* Involve marginalised users directly in design and testing, not just as a final review step
* Run an accessibility check against recognised standards (e.g. WCAG) before launch, not after complaints come in
* Test the product with people who have a range of abilities, devices, and levels of digital literacy
* Where possible, build in offline, low-bandwidth, or low-tech alternatives so people without reliable digital access are not further excluded
* Recruit and resource teams with diverse lived experience, so blindspots are caught during design, not after deployment
* Ask explicitly, at each design stage: "who might this harm or exclude, and how would we know?"



**Questions to ask yourself**&#x20;

* Who is not in the room and how would they describe the value and/or risks of this tool?
* If a user has no data plan, an older smartphone, or limited literacy, is this tool still functional or does it exclude them?
* Have we actively consulted with the most marginalised group likely to use this?
