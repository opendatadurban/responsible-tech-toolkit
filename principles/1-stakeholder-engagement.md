# (1) Question the problem before you build

Before writing a single line of code, ask whether the problem actually needs a new piece of technology to solve it. Is there an alternative solution required?&#x20;

If you do need to build, design technology that lasts. Leverage what already exists rather than starting from scratch each time.

* Start by asking whether the technology actually solves the problem, not just whether it's technically feasible
* Research the tools and tech stacks available before assuming something new needs to be built.&#x20;
* Don't reinvent the wheel. Build on top of existing products, tech stacks and communities wherever possible, rather than duplicating effort. &#x20;
* Prioritise open-source and reusable approaches so your work outlives any single team or organisation, and can be built on by others for other purposes
* Design for longevity. Plan for maintenance and handover from the start, so the technology remains usable after the original team moves on



**Questions to ask yourself:**&#x20;

* Am I building this because it's needed, or because it's possible?
* What tech stack or infrastructure do we already have that this could build on?
* Who will maintain this once the original team moves on?
* Will this still be usable and used in five years?
