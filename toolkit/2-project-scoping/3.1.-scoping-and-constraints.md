# 3.1. Scoping and constraints

{% stepper %}
{% step %}
### Identify user needs&#x20;

* As a team (including client, funder and partner as necessary), develop a detailed stakeholder map to identify all parties affected by or interested in the project. This should include marginalised or vulnerable groups whose voices might otherwise be overlooked.
* Categorise stakeholders by their level of influence and interest, plan targeted engagement activities for each group, and establish mechanisms for ongoing dialogue and feedback throughout the project lifecycle.
* Identify your core group of users. Conduct a comprehensive needs assessment to understand their specific challenges and opportunities. This could involve primary or secondary qualitative and quantitative research methods, such as surveys, interviews, and data analysis.
  * Where possible, sessions should be run with representative end users, prioritising marginalised or underserved groups
* Document and transparently share what was learned from user engagement activities. Use inclusive language and accessible formats to ensure broad participation.

{% hint style="info" icon="people-arrows" %}
A key attribute of identifying user needs within a responsible technology framework is ensuring that engaging users is non-extractive. So communities benefit more from the design and product they helped build! Or their lives and knowledge helped build! Co-liberation means that you understand how your flourishing and/ liberation is tied to marginalised communities. When you work with marginalised groups, you build trusting relationships that also create space for them to learn important skills that empower them, but also allow them to impart their knowledge and experiences in your process.
{% endhint %}

Tip: Users or beneficiaries of a tool often shape the eventual updates of the said tool. It is important to understand the cultures around a specific technology to know how it will impact the said group in the first place.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
### Frame the problem clearly&#x20;

* Develop a problem statement that clearly articulates the issue being addressed, avoiding assumptions about technology solutions from the outset. Remain open to findings that challenge initial assumptions about the problem or its solutions.
* Validate the problem framing with community and stakeholder input before locking scope

{% hint style="success" %}
Note: Communities affected by a problem find the most creative approaches to addressing them.
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="info" %}
A helpful resource to unpack the foundational problem that you're trying to address:&#x20;

* [How to identify the real problem](https://opencitieslab.gitbook.io/open-cities-lab-data-strategy-toolkit/resources/how-to-identify-the-real-problem)
* [How to construct a detailed problem statement ](https://opencitieslab.gitbook.io/open-cities-lab-data-strategy-toolkit/resources/how-to-construct-a-detailed-problem-statement)
{% endhint %}
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
### Map your product landscape&#x20;

1. **Understand what data you have and what you need**

Depending on the problem your problem might be trying to solve, it may be useful to establish a clear picture of the data that you have before any design or build decisions are made.&#x20;

Ask yourself the following:&#x20;

* What data already exists across the organisation?&#x20;
* What is currently being collected?
* What else should we collect to meet our objectives? &#x20;
* Who owns the data?&#x20;
* Why is it being gathered?&#x20;

Remember that this always needs to link back to the problem you're trying to solve. This audit also surfaces duplication, gaps, and risk early, and gives the team a clear starting point for brainstorming solutions.&#x20;



2. **Map any additional external constraints**&#x20;

Confirm the specific legal, regulatory and technical constraints in the country or context of deployment. Regulatory environments vary significantly across countries and it is essential that whatever you build aligns with the particularities of that specific country. You need to become very familiar with local data protection laws and other sector-specific rules in your jurisdiction(s). Be sure to understand these constraints upfront and document them clearly so that they genuinely inform design and deployment to avoid potential rebuilds later on. &#x20;
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
### Confirm brief&#x20;

Revisit the original problem statement in light of what the data audit, constraints mapping, and legal review have revealed, and adjust scope accordingly before moving forward. Early briefs are often written before the full picture is known, so treat this as a genuine checkpoint.&#x20;

Confirming the brief at this stage keeps the whole team (including partners, stakeholders and other users) aligned on a scope that's actually achievable. Have a session to take everyone through all the particularities that the research in this scoping phase has unearthed. It will all inform next steps and ensure a collective vision as work continues.&#x20;
{% endstep %}
{% endstepper %}
