# 3.3. Technical Scoping

The aim of this phase is to understand what is technically feasible to solve the problem. This means mapping the ambitions of the initial idea and design against the realities of existing infrastructure, team capability, and budget. If this research and planning isn't detailed enough, development can stall halfway through, because the product turns out to be impractical or unrealistic. This may ultimately force a rebuild or sourcing of additional funds, which is exactly what this phase exists to prevent.

{% stepper %}
{% step %}
### Consider what solutions already exist that could be used in this build

A key part of this work is checking whether open-source tools or existing solutions already solve the problem, in whole or in part. Building from scratch should be a deliberate choice, not a default.

{% hint style="info" %}
Remember some of the features of our very first Principle "[Question the problem before you build](../../principles/1-stakeholder-engagement.md)":&#x20;

* Research the tools and tech stacks available before assuming something new needs to be built.&#x20;
* Don't reinvent the wheel. Build on top of existing products, tech stacks and communities wherever possible, rather than duplicating effort. &#x20;
* Prioritise open-source and reusable approaches so your work outlives any single team or organisation, and can be built on by others for other purposes
{% endhint %}

If you have identified potential options, it's also crucial to not only understand its technical capabilities but also its broader context as that speaks to sustainability, access, privacy and other key factors to consider in your decision:&#x20;

* What open-source or existing solutions already exist to meet the needs of the project we're building?
  * Is the tech actively maintained? Who is maintaining it? Who funds the maintenance? How long into production has the software been used? Which community is supporting the software? What software licensed does it operate under (e.g. MIT)?&#x20;
  * How readily do components of this tech integrate with our other solutions? Consider technical debt and other dependencies in final decision-making.&#x20;
  * How does this support our broader tech strategy?&#x20;
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
### **Match scope to team capability, budget and organisational capacity.**&#x20;

This step requires a candid evaluation of technical skills, operational bandwidth, and financial constraints to plan product development effectively. Rather than overcommitting and relying on unsustainable developer effort, aligning scope with available resources allows teams to prioritise core value, account for realistic maintenance costs, and establish achievable timelines that preserve delivery quality and long-term momentum.

* Do we need specialised domain expertise?&#x20;
* Do we have capacity to take the project of this size on right now?&#x20;
* What mitigation strategies can we put in place to ensure smooth delivery?&#x20;
* What features do we need to put in the backlog or "out of scope" to align with resources available?&#x20;
{% endstep %}
{% endstepper %}

{% hint style="info" %}
Handle these decisions with care. Responsible technology means choosing the option that best serves the people the system is meant to help, not simply the option that's fastest to ship or most familiar to the team.&#x20;
{% endhint %}

Once the solution has been properly scoped and has buy-in from the whole team, a full rebuild during delivery should no longer be a realistic risk. This means that you can move on to the nuts and bolts of getting the product built.&#x20;
