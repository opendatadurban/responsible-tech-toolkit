# What is responsible technology?

Technology touches nearly every part of our lives. Yet it is often built and deployed without asking who it might harm or what risks it carries.

In practice, this looks like:

* Data is collected extractively, far beyond what's needed, with little thought for how it could be misused or breached
* Tools are built without consulting the people who are meant to use them so they sit unused - swallowing up resources with little to no impact&#x20;
* Accessibility and disability needs are an afterthought, if considered at all
* Systems trained on biased data or built without diverse input reinforce rather than reduce existing inequalities
* Environmental costs of infrastructure, devices and data storage go unmeasured and unmanaged
* Design choices optimise for engagement, efficiency and ultimately profit at the expense of people's wellbeing, contributing to addiction, anxiety and burnout
* Treating security and privacy as afterthoughts rather than core requirements, exposing users to systemic vulnerabilities that extend far beyond the app itself.
* Keeping core technical mechanisms obscure and opaque, creating a knowledge vacuum that bad actors and bad policy can easily exploit.

**The vision: What a responsible tech future could look like**

Identifying current harms is a straightforward exercise. But we also need to define the destination we are building toward.

Below are some elements of a future where technology is built and used responsibly:

* Technology is only developed or adopted when there is a clear, demonstrated need. Technology is not built for its own sake.&#x20;
* The people affected by a technology, especially those from marginalised or underserved groups, are consulted early and often, shaping the design rather than reacting to it after the fact
* Privacy, security and data minimisation are built in from the first line of code or first product decision not retrofitted after a breach or complaint
* Accessibility is a baseline requirement, not an optional add-on, ensuring tools work for people of all abilities
* Environmental impact is weighed alongside cost and functionality when choosing infrastructure, hardware and tools
* Design choices respect people's time, attention and mental health, rather than exploiting them for profit
* Clear ownership and accountability ensure someone is responsible for the consequences of what gets built
* Technology is tested for bias and disparate impact before release, with mechanisms for people to contest or appeal automated decisions

Ultimately, this shift moves us from technology that is imposed and extractive to technology that is trusted, inclusive and genuinely useful.
