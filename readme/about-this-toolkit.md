# About this toolkit

The toolkit empowers individuals and teams to ask crucial questions throughout the tech development lifecycle. It provides the frameworks and resources to assess potential risks, biases and other issues before they become immutable problems.&#x20;



* ### Who made this?

This toolkit was developed by Mardiya Siba Yahaya through the Urban Resilience Programme with the support of the UK Government. Mardiya Siba Yahaya  is a Gender Equality and Social Inclusion expert and lead the development of Toolkit V1.0 with contributions from key members of her team. OCL’s internal Responsible Tech Team led the revisions to Toolkit V2.0 with many thanks to Sarah Findlay, Afia Akosah-Bempah, Paul Figueira, Brian O'Leary and Keotshepile Maje with oversight and support from Wasim Moosa and Joanne Parker. [Claude.ai](http://claude.ai) was used in some places to support specific rephrasing for readability.&#x20;



* ### Why we made it?

We build technology for a living which means we're just as capable of falling into the traps this toolkit warns against as anyone else. We've built things without asking enough questions first, made assumptions about who our tools were really for, and only spotted our own blindspots after the fact, when it was harder to fix. We realised that if we wanted to hold others to a responsible standard, we needed a mirror for ourselves first: a way to catch our own biases, gaps and shortcuts before they became someone else's problem.

This toolkit therefore isn't something we wrote from the outside looking in. It's the thing that we commissioned because we needed it and didn't have: a practical way to interrogate our own decisions, built from our own mistakes and near-misses, that we now use ourselves and share so others don't have to learn the same lessons the hard way.



* ### Why did we choose this platform?&#x20;

We use GitBook as it is a trusted open source tool with an intuitive interface and is relatively easy to replicate using the "fork command".

It also allows the output from GitBook to be pushed to GitHub, stored as .md (markdown) and redeployed as HTML on a your own website in your own domain.
