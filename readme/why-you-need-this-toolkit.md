# Why you need this toolkit

Many organisations have already taken important first steps. Perhaps you have an IT policy, a privacy lead, or a digital product team. However, these may operate as isolated safeguards that don't add up to a coherent approach.

What you need is a well-designed set of principles that ensures your teams ask the right questions at every stage.&#x20;

By working through the exercises in this toolkit, you can make sure that your team is equipped to:&#x20;

* Ask whether a piece of technology should be built at all (before asking how)
* Bring in marginalised and underserved voices from the start, not as an afterthought
* Assess risks from privacy, to equity, to accessibility, to the environment before they become harms
* Build in practices that build accountability and transparency&#x20;
* Create technology that people actually want to use, and can trust

With the right principles in place, technology can become a genuine force for equity and public good rather than a source of new harm.

{% hint style="info" icon="lightbulb-exclamation-on" %}
Building responsible technology isn't just about ethics. It's also good business. Incorporating security, equity, and sustainability from the start prevents costly redesigns, protects investor confidence, and preserves user trust that takes years to build. Practical choices, such as optimising code and data pipelines, also slash real-world water and electricity bills for server infrastructure. Ultimately, responsible tech is a practical strategy for saving money, reducing risk, and ensuring long-term resilience.
{% endhint %}
